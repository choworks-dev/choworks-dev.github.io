You have probably received a quote for a website. I have received a few. And every time I stalled at the same place. I could see the numbers. I could not see what the numbers were for.

## What a quote does not tell you

The quotes I got mostly looked like this.

```
Planning and scoping     $X,000
Design                   $X,000
Build                    $X,000
Maintenance (per year)     $X00
```

The only thing this table lets me decide is whether I can afford the total. Which line is actually necessary, what happens if I remove one, what stops working if I do. None of that is in there.

So I did what most people do. Got two or three quotes, picked the cheapest, or put it off. **I put it off for years.**

## Three different kinds of work are mixed in there

Looking back, the problem with that table is that it is split by **production stage, not by kind of work**. To decide as an owner you need a different split.

**Moving things.** Taking what exists and putting it somewhere else. Hosting, domains, porting existing pages. There is a correct answer, and you can tell immediately whether you got it.

**Making things.** Creating what did not exist. Deciding what to show, in what order, and turning that into something people look at. **There is no correct answer.**

**Keeping things.** Everything after launch. Fixing typos, changing prices, swapping photos. Small amounts of work with no end date.

## Moving things is now something you can do yourself

I [moved two sites off Wix in two days using Claude and Cursor](/en/posts/wix-to-cloudflare/). About $400 a year went to zero.

Not because I learned the technology. I still do not really understand DNS. What changed is that **there is now something I can ask whenever I get stuck**, and moving things is the kind of work where asking produces an answer.

Let me not oversell this. Two days means two days of ten-plus hours. Fit around a real job it is closer to a week. But that turns it into a choice between **a few thousand dollars and a week of your time.** It was not a choice before.

**Keeping things changed even more.** Emailing an agency about a typo and waiting for a reply became: open the file, fix it, save. Moving happens once. Keeping happens every month.

## What you should still hire out

**Making things is different.** Deciding what to show has no correct answer, and questions with no correct answer do not get answered by asking. AI builds what I decided. It does not tell me what to decide.

And there is a category you should not touch yourself.

Payments. Anywhere card details pass through, a mistake ends the business. Same for collecting and storing personal data. Same for **anything where being wrong means money is wrong**, like bookings or inventory.

These three are not a question of whether you can. They are a question of who is liable when it breaks. I do not do these myself.

## The one thing to decide before asking for quotes

Before you pick a vendor, settle this.

**Can I open the result myself?**

I once [rebuilt an automation that already worked perfectly well](/en/posts/google-apps-script-to-n8n/). It worked, but nobody except the person who built it could open it.

Websites are the same. If it is finished and beautiful but changing one word means emailing the agency, **that is a website that will be abandoned again in a few years.** Mine was.

So put these questions in your request.

- After launch, can I edit text and images myself?
- Is there a screen for that, or do I have to touch files?
- If I move to another vendor later, what exactly do I take with me?

**If the third question gets a vague answer, look at that quote again.**

## To summarize

| | Now | |
|---|---|---|
| Moving things | **You can do it** | Correct answers exist, so asking works |
| Keeping things | **You can do it** | This is the bigger change |
| Making things | Hire | Nobody tells you what to show |
| Payments, personal data | Hire | Liability lives here |

This is not an argument against hiring. It is an argument that **once you split what is bundled inside the quote, the same budget buys only what you actually need.**

If your website has sat untouched for years, that is probably not laziness. It is that **you could not get a price on the work, so it never got picked up.** Mine was like that. Now the pieces have prices.
