Website quotes do not look the way they used to. I have been on the receiving end of those quotes, and these days I write them too, so I see the change from inside. Here is what is useful to know from both sides.

## Prices really did drop

This is not a bubble deflating. It is real, and the reason is simple. **Building screens takes less time now.**

A design pass used to take days. Now it takes hours. Same for making it work on phones, chasing browser differences, wiring up forms. **The work did not disappear. The hours it consumes did.**

Less time means a lower price. So if today's quote looks different from the one you got three years ago, nothing suspicious is happening.

## But something did not drop

This is the part that matters.

```
Dropped        the time to build
Did not drop   the time to decide
```

How many times someone looks at a design and says **"that is not it"**. AI does not shrink that. What you want lives inside the person asking, not the person building, and asking does not extract it.

**It can even grow.** Building faster means producing more options, and more options means slower decisions. You used to choose between two. Now you sit in front of six.

So most of the variance in a modern quote comes from **the deciding, not the building.**

## What I look at before quoting

Whether to take a job, and what number to put on it, comes down to four things.

**One. Is it decided what to build?**

"Something clean and modern" is not decided. **Two or three sites you like** is decided. With those, the first draft does not miss badly.

**Two. Is there one decision maker?**

One owner deciding is fast. Owner, spouse, staff member, and a friend in the business all weighing in means the design goes around forever. **People like different things, and nobody is wrong, so there is nothing to resolve.**

**Three. Who provides the words and pictures?**

This is the most common source of delay by a wide margin. The shell takes two days and then two months pass because **there is no copy to put in it.**

**Four. Is the revision scope written down?**

If it is not, both sides get hurt. The client thinks a change is obviously included. The builder thinks it is extra. Both are being sincere.

## How the number gets set

I split it like this.

| | Now | |
|---|---|---|
| Building | **Down a lot** | Fewer hours |
| Deciding | **Unchanged** | It is human decision time |
| Maintaining | **Nearly gone** | If you can edit it yourself |

And I write **a revision count** into the quote. Something like "three rounds included after design sign-off, then a fixed fee per round."

**I do not offer unlimited revisions.** Priced cheaply, unlimited hurts me. Priced with all that risk loaded in, the quote does not win. What actually happens is that both sides write "unlimited" and then tiptoe around each other, which is the worst outcome.

**A quote with a revision count is better for the client too.** With that number in place I carry less risk, so the total comes down.

## For clients: how to pay less

Better than negotiating the number down. **Reduce how many rounds it will take.**

- **Send three sites you like.** One line each on why is even better
- **Name one decision maker.** Many people can have opinions; one person decides
- **Prepare the words and pictures first.** They do not have to be final. A draft is enough

With those three in place, quotes genuinely come down. Not out of generosity, but because **the risk I have to price in shrinks.** A large part of any quote is insurance against "no idea how many rounds this will take."

## Some prices should not drop

Payments. Anywhere card details pass through, one mistake ends a business. Same for collecting and storing personal data. Same for **anything where being wrong means money is wrong**, like bookings or inventory.

These did not get cheaper and should not. It is **not a question of whether someone can build it, but who is liable when it breaks.** A quote where these lines look unusually cheap may be one where nobody is carrying that liability.

## Moving and maintaining, you can do yourself

Apart from building, **moving** is now something you can handle. I [moved two sites off Wix in two days with Claude and Cursor](/en/posts/wix-to-cloudflare/). About $400 a year went to zero.

Moving has correct answers, so asking when you get stuck produces one. **Maintaining is the same.** Emailing someone about a typo and waiting became: open the file, fix it, save.

So put these questions in your request.

- After launch, can I edit text and images myself?
- Is there a screen for that?
- If I move to another vendor later, what exactly do I take with me?

**If the third answer is vague, look at that quote again.** [A result nobody but its author can open gets abandoned in a few years.](/en/posts/google-apps-script-to-n8n/) Mine was.

## To summarize

AI did lower the price. But **not evenly.**

```
Building        down a lot
Deciding        unchanged.  the revision count is this number
Maintaining     nearly gone
Liability       unchanged.  and it should stay that way
```

Split any quote along those four lines and you can see where cutting is safe and where cutting costs more later.

And **preparing beats negotiating.** Three reference sites and one decision maker move the total more than any line item in the quote does.
